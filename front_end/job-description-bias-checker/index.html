What the checker looks for

  • Masculine-coded words. "Dominant", "competitive", "fearless", "assertive" and about 40 more from Gaucher, Friesen and Kay (2011).
  • Feminine-coded words. "Nurturing", "collaborative", "supportive", "empathetic" and others from the same study.
  • Age-coded phrases. "Digital native", "recent graduate", "young and dynamic", "high energy".
  • Exclusionary terms. "Native English speaker", "culture fit", "salesman", "guys", "he or she".
  • Hype and filler. "Rockstar", "ninja", "self-starter", "wear many hats", "work hard play hard".
  • Missing pay. No salary, pay range or hourly rate found anywhere in the text.

Why wording matters

Job ads are often the first thing a candidate reads about you. Small word choices change who feels the job is for them. In a series of studies, Gaucher, Friesen and Kay (2011) found that ads with more masculine-coded words were rated less appealing by women, even when the job itself was the same. The effect came from a sense of not belonging, not from doubts about skill.

Age works the same way. Phrases like "digital native" or "young team" tell experienced people to look elsewhere, and in many countries they can also raise legal risk. Terms like "native English speaker" can screen out fluent speakers for no job-related reason. Better: "fluent in written and spoken English".

Hype words are less harmful but cost you clarity. "Rockstar engineer in a fast-paced environment" tells a candidate nothing. "Ship features to 40,000 users every two weeks" tells them a lot.

Frequently asked questions

What is gender-coded language in a job description?

Words that research links to one gender, such as "dominant" or "competitive" (masculine-coded) and "nurturing" or "collaborative" (feminine-coded). A 2011 study by Gaucher, Friesen and Kay found that job ads heavy in masculine-coded words made the jobs less appealing to women.

Is a gender-coded word always wrong?

No. A single word like "active" or "support" is fine. This checker only flags gender wording when one side clearly dominates: 3 or more coded words and at least twice as many as the other side. Use it as a prompt to review, not a verdict.

Is my job description stored or sent anywhere?

No. The check runs entirely in your browser with plain word lists. The text you paste is not sent to a server or an AI.

What is age-coded language?

Phrases that signal a preferred age, such as "digital native", "recent graduate" or "young and dynamic". Studies of ageist language in job ads found such wording discourages older applicants, and it can raise age discrimination risk.

Does this replace a legal review?

No. It is a screening aid for wording. Laws on job ads and pay transparency differ by place and change often. Check with HR or legal counsel for compliance.
