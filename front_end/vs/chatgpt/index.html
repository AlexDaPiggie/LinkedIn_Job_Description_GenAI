ChatGPT vs a Job Description Generator

ChatGPT can write a job description. So can a dedicated tool. The difference is in what you have to check afterwards. Here is an honest comparison, including when ChatGPT is the better choice.

Short answer

Use ChatGPT if you write a job post once in a while, enjoy tweaking prompts, and will carefully review every line yourself.

Use a job description generator if you write job posts often, want the same format every time, and do not want to double-check for invented requirements, made-up pay or biased wording.

The 3 problems to watch for with ChatGPT

  1. 01

    Made-up details

    Ask for a "Senior Data Analyst job description" with no other facts and you get a confident list of tools, years and degrees that may not match your team. Each one narrows your candidate pool. Some can even create legal problems, like a salary you never approved.

  2. 02

    Inconsistent format

    Different hiring managers write different prompts, so every job post looks different. Candidates comparing roles at your company notice, and so does your brand.

  3. 03

    Generic, coded wording

    Language models lean on the phrases most common in job ads online, such as "fast-paced environment", "self-starter" and "rockstar". Those are the same words that make an ad sound generic and, in some cases, put off women or older applicants.

If you use ChatGPT, use a better prompt

This prompt fixes most of the problems above. Fill in the brackets.

Prompt 
Write a job description for LinkedIn.

Use ONLY the facts below. Do not add tools, degrees,
years of experience or pay that are not listed.
If something important is missing, list it at the end
as a question instead of guessing.

Role: [title], [seniority], [remote/hybrid/on-site, location]
Team: [team size, who they report to]
What they will do: [3-6 concrete duties]
Must have: [3-5 real requirements]
Nice to have: [1-3 items]
Benefits: [list]
Pay: [range, or "do not mention pay"]

Sections: About us, About the role, What you will do,
What you bring, Nice to have, Benefits, Pay, Equal
opportunity statement.

Avoid: rockstar, ninja, fast-paced, self-starter,
digital native, recent graduate, native speaker,
culture fit. Keep it under 600 words, short bullets.

Frequently asked questions

Can ChatGPT write a good job description?

Yes, if you give it the facts. With a detailed prompt it can write a solid draft. The common problems are made-up requirements or pay, a different structure each time, and wording that is generic or biased unless you ask it to check.

Why use a job description generator instead of ChatGPT?

A dedicated generator asks for the facts a job post needs, keeps the same structure every time, never invents pay, flags requirements you did not give, and runs a fixed bias and wording check. That saves prompt work and review time, especially when you write many job posts.

Is ChatGPT free for job descriptions?

ChatGPT has a free plan with usage limits and paid plans. The LinkedInGenAI generator is free to write, check, copy and export with no account. Only custom refine requests use credits.

Can I use both?

Yes. Many people generate a structured draft with a dedicated tool, then use ChatGPT for extra tasks like interview questions or an outreach message. You can also paste a ChatGPT draft into the free bias checker.
